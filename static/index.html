Medical Reference & Anemia Classifications

A clinician's guide to red blood cell indexes and morphologic classifications of anemia.

1. Understanding Red Blood Cell Indices

Complete Blood Counts (CBC) provide diagnostic indices crucial for classifying anemia and identifying its primary etiology.

Hemoglobin (Hb)

The oxygen-transporting protein in RBCs. It serves as the primary diagnostic threshold for anemia.

  • Normal Male: 13.5 – 17.5 g/dL
  • Normal Female: 12.0 – 15.5 g/dL

Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)

The average volume (size) of red blood cells. Categorizes anemia into microcytic, normocytic, or macrocytic.

  • Microcytic: < 80 fL
  • Normocytic: 80 – 100 fL
  • Macrocytic: > 100 fL

Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH)

The average mass of hemoglobin per red blood cell. Closely tracks MCV.

  • Normal Range: 27 – 33 pg
  • Low (Hypochromic): < 27 pg

2. Morphologic Classifications of Anemia

Microcytic Hypochromic Anemia MCV < 80 fL | MCH < 27 pg

Characterized by small, pale red blood cells. This state indicates a defect in hemoglobin synthesis.

  • Primary Causes: Iron Deficiency Anemia (most common), Thalassemia, Sideroblastic Anemia, Lead Poisoning.
  • Recommended Workup: Serum Ferritin, Iron, Total Iron Binding Capacity (TIBC), Transferrin Saturation.
Macrocytic Anemia MCV > 100 fL

Characterized by abnormally large red blood cells. Divided into Megaloblastic (impaired DNA synthesis) and Non-Megaloblastic.

  • Primary Causes: Vitamin B12 Deficiency (Pernicious Anemia), Folate Deficiency, Alcoholism, Liver Disease, Drug-induced.
  • Recommended Workup: Serum B12, Serum/RBC Folate, Methylmalonic Acid (MMA) levels.
Normocytic Normochromic Anemia MCV 80 - 100 fL | MCH 27 - 33 pg

Red blood cells are normal in size and hemoglobin concentration, but overall count is depleted.

  • Primary Causes: Anemia of Chronic Disease (Infection, Inflammation, Malignancy), Acute Blood Loss, Hemolytic Anemia, Chronic Renal Failure.
  • Recommended Workup: Reticulocyte count (to assess bone marrow response), Renal panel (EPO levels), CRP/ESR.
